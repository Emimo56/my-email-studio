# The Steep Club — Launch Campaign Plan

Planning document only. No emails go out from this plan — see studio rules.

## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** Steep Club Launch — Waitlist to Subscriber

**Summary:** A 4-week, email-only campaign inviting our ~2,000 waitlist members to become the first subscribers to The Steep Club.

**Primary objective:** 300 waitlist members start a Steep Club subscription within the first month (15% of the waitlist).

**Secondary objectives:**
- Keep unsubscribe rate under 2% across the sequence.
- Build a base of early subscribers who can give feedback on the first few boxes.
- Learn which of the three audience groups converts best, to guide future campaigns.

## 2. Target Audience

Three segments, all already on the waitlist (~2,000 people total). Exact group sizes aren't known yet — worth asking whoever manages the waitlist for a breakdown by signup source or quiz answer, if one exists.

**1. Tea-curious beginners**
- Interested in tea but unsure where to start or what "good" tea tastes like.
- Pain point: overwhelmed by choice, worried about wasting money on something they won't like.
- Motivation: wants an easy, low-risk way to explore.
- Buying stage: consideration — needs reassurance more than persuasion.

**2. Gift buyers**
- Joined the waitlist thinking of The Steep Club as a gift for someone else (or were gifted a sample).
- Pain point: wants something that feels thoughtful, not generic.
- Motivation: a gift that doesn't feel like "just another basket."
- Buying stage: consideration — may convert for themselves, or as a gift purchase.

**3. Daily tea drinkers**
- Already drink tea regularly, likely buy loose leaf or bagged tea elsewhere.
- Pain point: wants reliably good tea without constantly researching or reordering.
- Motivation: quality and consistency; cares about sourcing and freshness.
- Buying stage: closer to decision — needs specifics (what's in the box, how sourcing works, how delivery works).

All three groups are already warm — they opted in and asked to hear from us. The job of this campaign is to turn interest into a first subscription, not to build awareness from scratch.

## 3. Key Messages

**Core message:** The wait is over — The Steep Club is here, and you get first access.

**Supporting messages (by segment):**

| Segment | Supporting message |
|---|---|
| Tea-curious beginners | Simple, loose-leaf tea, chosen for you each month — no guesswork. |
| Gift buyers | A small-batch box that feels personal, whether it's for you or someone else. |
| Daily tea drinkers | Honest sourcing, loose leaf, delivered on a schedule you can count on. |

**Proof points to use:** what the tea is (loose leaf, small batch), where it comes from, how it's packaged, what's actually in the first box. No claims about taste, health, or results — per the brand voice guide, we describe what things are, not what they do.

**Message hierarchy for each email:**
1. Why this matters to you (your reason for joining the waitlist)
2. What The Steep Club actually is
3. Why now (first-access window, limited first batch — framed honestly, not as pressure)
4. What to do next (start your subscription)

## 4. Channel Strategy

Email only, per budget. No paid ads, no social spend.

| Channel | Why it fits | Format | Effort | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Email (owned) | Audience already opted in; zero media cost; full control of tone and timing | 5-email sequence, segmented by group where possible | Medium | $0 media spend; cost is writing/design/send-platform time only |

If segmentation data isn't available in the email tool, send one sequence to everyone but vary a section or two to speak to each group (see Content Pieces, below).

## 7. Success Metrics

**Primary KPI:** 300 new subscriptions started by end of Week 4 (15% of ~2,000).

**Secondary KPIs:**
- Open rate per email (benchmark: compare Email 1 vs. Email 6 to see drop-off)
- Click-through rate to the sign-up page
- Sign-up rate by segment (beginners vs. gift buyers vs. daily drinkers)
- Unsubscribe rate (target: under 2% across the sequence)
- List growth/decay (how many people disengage entirely)

**Tracking:** email platform's own open/click/conversion reporting, plus a simple count of new subscriptions tagged "waitlist launch" in whatever system tracks subscriptions.

**Reporting cadence:** quick check after Email 2 (mid-Week 1/2) to catch problems early; full report at end of Week 4.

## 8. Budget Allocation

Budget is small and email-only, so there's no media spend to allocate. The real cost is time:

- Copywriting (6 emails, segment variants): most of the effort
- Design/formatting: light, assuming an existing email template
- List segmentation setup: one-time task in Week 0
- Contingency: keep a few hours free in Week 2 in case Email 4 or 5 needs rework based on early results

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Segmentation data doesn't exist, so all three groups get identical emails | Fall back to one sequence with a short "pick what applies to you" block instead of true segmentation |
| Early emails underperform and 300 looks out of reach by Week 2 | Use the Week 2 review point to adjust Email 4/5 messaging or add a bonus email, rather than waiting until Week 4 to react |
| Urgency messaging in Emails 5–6 drifts into hype language | Have every email checked against `brand/voice.md` before sending — specifically the words-we-avoid list |
